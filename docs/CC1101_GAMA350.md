# CC1101 868 MHz + GAMA 350: ESPHome Wireless M-Bus technical notes

This guide documents the **ESP32 + CC1101** receiver used to read the **GAMA 350 / GAMA350 smart electricity meter** through Wireless M-Bus (wM-Bus), with an **Apator water meter** on the same receiver.

## Confirmed hardware and firmware

- ESP32 DevKit with ESP-IDF, ESPHome **2026.10.1**.
- CC1101 868 MHz receiver using SPI: SCK GPIO18, MOSI GPIO23, MISO GPIO19, CS GPIO15; GDO0/IRQ GPIO4.
- Verified radio implementation: v4.9, commit `11b09de7f87ade632453f772c37940dbdb470c94`, source `experiment/v4.9-stable-marcstate-eof`.
- Upstream source pinned to `a732dd0e52c48973fad57e4950a11d8e2f2d6820`, plus eight local patches.
- GAMA driver: `amiplus`; Apator driver: `apatorna1`.
- Experimentally selected receive frequency: **868.970 MHz**.
- CC1101 RX gain boosted, normal sync and 16-byte FIFO IRQ threshold in the validated patch series.

## Long GAMA frames and 3-of-6 coding

A full GAMA RF capture is typically **326 bytes** in the tested installation. Distinguish these counts:

1. A detected radio event or IRQ notification is not automatically a valid telegram.
2. A full 326-byte CC1101 capture may still fail `3of6` decoding.
3. A decoded GAMA telegram produces the ESPHome kWh, tariff and power sensor updates.

Useful log patterns:

```text
RF_DIAG bytes=326 freqest_raw=... lqi_raw=... marcstate=0x0D
Long RX: bytes=326 bursts=... peak_fifo=... ms=... rssi=... unstable_rxbytes=0
Telegram ... electricity meter received
Long frame 3of6 error: encoded_byte=... symbol=...
RX_DIAG cumulative ... payload_fail=... queue_drops=...
```

The last two lines are examples of **different** error categories. `lqi_raw` includes the CC1101 CRC_OK bit and therefore should not be interpreted as a pure 0–255 LQI scale. Raw `freqest` describes a receiver estimate, not an independently calibrated transmitter frequency. Nominal 30-second GAMA windows are a statistical approximation, not confirmed broadcasts.

## Real-world reception trial (October 29, 2026)

The hardware receiver accidentally fell to the garage floor, producing sharply worse reception. After remounting approximately **80 cm above the floor**, the GAMA RSSI improved by roughly **22 dB** and long-frame decoding recovered to approximately 99% of received full frames.

A controlled tuning comparison then changed only the radio frequency while keeping hardware placement fixed:

| Stage | Frequency | Full GAMA frames | Correctly decoded | 3of6 failures | Decoded / full |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A1 | 868.970 MHz | 278 | 276 | 2 | 99.28% |
| B | 868.950 MHz | 100 | 99 | 1 | 99.00% |
| A2 | 868.970 MHz | 174 | 172 | 2 | 98.85% |

These trials have different observation durations and do **not** demonstrate a statistically meaningful decoding advantage of one frequency. FREQEST was more favorable at 868.970 MHz, which was retained as the configuration preference. RSSI stayed near −59 dBm on the wall. Short Apator telegrams continued to decode during the tests.

**Do not confuse complete-frame decoding success with reception of every transmission.** Without a second calibrated receiver, we cannot prove how many telegrams the meter sent but the ESP32 never captured.

## Diagnostic priorities

- For poor 3of6 decoding, inspect physical receiver placement, antenna orientation, obstacles and RSSI before changing frequency or CC1101 internals.
- For `RX FIFO overflow`, `RXBYTES` or unexpectedly missing 326-byte frames, confirm you are using the validated v4.9 source and not an experimental branch.
- For isolated `RX timeout after 0 bytes` events, check concurrent Apator traffic and cumulative error counters; they are not automatically proven lost water telegrams.
- Watch `queue_drops` and `unstable_rxbytes`. No errors in these counters is reassuring, but not proof every transmitted packet was captured.
- Avoid publishing raw telegrams, real meter IDs, network identifiers or decryption keys.
